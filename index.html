Welcome to My Web Page

I really enjoyed the type of work that was shown by Sara Stern. I was able to tell that she enjoys using the environment as an addition to her artwork. The first artwork that was shown was very interesting as I honestly didn’t know about the history of the Columbiana University’s new building. Her work of being able to turn architecture into a form of art by combining it with puppets really impressed me, I never thought about art in that form before. It links back into the trend about transplants and gentrification, and how people dislike having new people moving into the city or creation of new modern buildings in places that they either went to or grew up with. Having the building be able to speak also gave it more sympathy, like having a soul.

The second one I want to talk about is Mirrorball. Not only that I thought that the movement and the puppet design were very creative, I also thought that it was a bit silly; mainly due to the movement of the puppets. Stern continues to use the environment—in this case, the dunes—to build upon the art. The use of the instrument was also important as it gave it an idea similar to old school video games and their “desert-ish” original sound track. I also think that Mirrorball is a great representation of queer history. The two dolls with identical clothing and halve disco balls for head could represent how generally gay men (assuming that they are masculine) share the exact same mannerisms, fashion sense, or interests.
