Nuclear Conflict article

Honestly, at first I was wondering why we would have to read an article about the risk of Nuclear War and how it relates to the subject that we are doing as media studies. But as I looked at the website and scrolled down and read the article, nothing prepared me for how immersive the whole article was with its graphic design, typography, artwork, and animation.

I really like how part of the article starts off with a black background with simple white text on the center of the screen; it really grabs a person’s attention. The red fading in from the background also adds subtle improvement too; it gives the article a sense of urgency on how important and serious the topic is. It also looks like an alarm that is going off. Then it fades from the black background to the standard white background that the New York Times uses for all its articles. This pattern continues later on—the switch between the black and white background. The black background shows the horror of a nuclear weapon and what tragedies could happen if a nuclear war does break out. The white background refers to the information or stories that are being written about—similarily to any newspaper article. Along with the white background, the formatting and typography used is the same to every single NYT article. This tells us that even when some parts of the article are different; it’s still a New York Times article and it still belongs here.

The artwork and animation also helps with this immersiveness. I really like how each scroll down the page shows a different frame of animation in the article. The color mainly being Red, orange, yellow, and black gives it not only a sense of warmth but also a sense of danger—with the warmth color being used as fire while black is shown to be an object that is burnt. The composition in each frame of the animation really shows the peril of nuclear weapons by displaying how chaotic it can be when the government does use them. Along with the artwork and animation, the small textbox also helps as it describes the scene of what is happening—like a narration. The zoning in, for each frame of animation also helps with the immersiveness, it feels like that you are part of the story or part of the event that is currently happening. The artstyle being more realistic than cartoony also helps as it conveys the message that it is reality that we live in and the danger of using nuclear weapons could happen. Back to the "newspaper" part (white background), the design for it is very organized. The images/gifs that were used are organized into simple boxes—like a window. Some composition of the photos used in this article makes it very easy to understand. The infographics also help make it clear and straightforward.

I would have never read this article if there were no graphic design, typography, artwork, or animation. It shows how important these elements are when making a page due to their ability of being able to immerse the reader into the world that the writer wants to show.
